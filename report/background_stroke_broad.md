### **Variation across sociodemographic groups and hospitals in Acute Ischaemic Stroke management: a cohort study using real-world data from UK hospitals**

#### Abstract

**Background**

Despite the acute management of AIS being well-established in both European and UK guidelines (1,2), several observational studies have raised concerns about disparities in the healthcare delivered in this setting across population groups, particularly with respect to age, sex, ethnicity, and socioeconomic status (SES) (3–6). However, many of these studies have focused on a single aspect of treatment and/or a limited range of sociodemographic factors. We aimed to characterise healthcare processes for AIS across UK hospital settings, focusing on populations at higher risk of healthcare inequities.

**Methods**

We conducted a network cohort study across the UK using routinely collected electronic health records mapped to the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership Common Data Model (OMOP CDM). The study included three datasets within the Health Data Research UK OMOP Network (HERON UK) (7): Barts Health NHS Trusts (Barts), Lancashire Teaching Hospitals (IDRIL), and Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust (LTHT).  The study period was from 2022 to end of database capture. Individuals were required to be aged 18 years or older, to be admitted as an inpatient, and to have a hospital record of AIS, which was required to be the first in that hospital and which was established as the index event. The primary outcomes of interest were in-hospital drug treatment and/or procedures implemented in the 28 days after the AIS. Drug groups of interest were thrombolytics, antiplatelets, anticoagulants, and lipid-lowering drugs. Procedures were defined as “endovascular procedures related to stroke” (EPS), which included endovascular thrombectomy and extracranial or intracranial stenting in the acute setting. Individuals’ characteristics at the time of AIS were described, and included age, sex, SES, ethnicity, main comorbidities, and 28-days mortality. Additionally, the odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence interval (CI) to initiate each specific drug based on the sociodemographic characteristics was calculated, taking as a reference group the one with the highest proportion of individuals.

**Results**

15,358 individuals with a diagnosis of AIS were identified, 6,732 at Barts, 4,829 at IDRIL, and 3,797 at LTHT.  

Individuals at Barts were younger than individuals at IDRIL and LTHT (median age 69 years [58 - 80] vs 73 years [60 - 81] and 76 years [64-85], respectively).  A male predominance was observed at the three centres (52-56.1%). The most frequent ethnic group was White. Barts had the greatest ethnic diversity (25.6% Asian, 18% Black). IDRIL had the highest proportion of individuals from the two most deprived quintiles (Q4 and Q5) (22.4% and 22.1% respectively). Females and white individuals were older. The most frequent comorbidities were hypertension (61.7-70.9%), dyslipidaemia (30.7-46.2%) and diabetes mellitus type 2 (23-37.6%). 

28 days mortality was 8% at Barts, 8.2% at IDRIL, and 12.3% at LTHT. Mortality increased with increasing age, from 4.4-4.9% (<65 years) to 15.3-23.2% (>85 years). Mortality was higher in females than in males (8.5-14.2% vs 7.8-10.6%) and among Whites compared to Black (<5-12.3%) and Asian (4.6-11%) individuals. 

Antiplatelet therapy was prescribed in 68.6-90.6%. Anticoagulants were prescribed in 23.8-49.6%. Anticoagulation increased with increasing age across centres (from 14.4-40.9 % in <65 years to 34.2–54.5% in >85 years). Individuals in the lowest SES quintiles were prescribed more anticoagulants than individuals in the highest SES quintiles (25.6-49.2% vs 21.1-37.1%). 

Statins were prescribed to 52.4-78% of the study population, whereas ezetimibe was prescribed to 2.3-3%. Statins use declined in the elderly (≥85 years) as compared to the 65-84 years group ((52-83% vs 61-62.3%). Statins prescription was lower in females than in males (47.2-52.8% vs 56-57.2%). Thrombolytics were administered in 6.7% of cases at Barts and 3.3% at IDRIL.  Barts was the only centre in which we had data regarding EPS in the setting of AIS. 4% of AIS cases in such centre underwent EPS. The percentage of EPS was higher among women (4.5%) as compared to men (3.6%). The percentage of EPS performed declined with increasing age from 4.7% in <65 years old to 2.3% in >85 years old. The percentages of EPS were lower in Asian (2.7%) and Black (4.1%) individuals than in White individuals (4.5%).

**Discussion and conclusion**

Overall, the proportion of individuals receiving the analysed treatments would have been expected to be higher. This is particularly true for the treatments used for the hyperacute management of stroke (thrombolytics and EPS). This might be related to the limitations inherent to the use of RWD. Differences in the distribution of baseline clinical features, including median age and comorbidities, may partly explain the variation observed in the treatments prescribed in the setting of AIS across centers and sociodemographic subgroups. However, there were variations between population subgroups, such as sex and ethnic disparities in the prescription of statins, that raise the possibility of inequities in healthcare provision and that should be further explored in future studies specifically designed for that purpose.
