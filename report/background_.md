## MI/Stroke inpatient study

## Abstract

**Background and Aims**

Variations in the acute management of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) have been reported across population subgroups in Europe and the UK, yet a comprehensive real-world picture of these inequities remains lacking. This study characterised healthcare processes for AIS across UK hospital settings, with a focus on populations at higher risk of healthcare inequities.

**Methods**

A network cohort study was conducted using routinely collected electronic health records mapped to the OMOP Common Data Model across four UK hospital datasets: Barts Health NHS Trust, Lancashire Teaching Hospitals, Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, and DataLoch. Adults aged 18 or older with an inpatient record of first-ever AIS between 2022 and end of database capture were included. Primary outcomes were in-hospital pharmacological treatments (thrombolytics, antiplatelets, anticoagulants, and lipid-lowering agents) and endovascular procedures related to stroke (EPS) within 28 days of the index event.

**Results**

Results were available for two data partners (Barts and LTHT). Male patients predominated (52–56%), and hypertension, dyslipidaemia, and type 2 diabetes were the most common comorbidities. Twenty-eight-day mortality was 9.8% at Barts and 12.3% at LTHT, and was consistently higher in women and in more deprived groups. Thrombolytics were administered in 8.4% of Barts cases, with lower use in women and those aged over 85. Antiplatelet therapy was prescribed in the majority of patients (81.2–90.6%). Anticoagulant use increased with age and with greater socioeconomic deprivation. Statins were prescribed to 69.5–78% of patients, with lower rates in women and in older age groups. EPS rates were higher in women and in the least deprived patients.

**Conclusions**

Treatment rates for thrombolytics, EPS, and statins were lower than expected, particularly in older patients. Sex-based disparities in thrombolytic and statin prescribing, and socioeconomic gradients in EPS access, raise concerns about potential inequities in AIS care that warrant further investigation.
